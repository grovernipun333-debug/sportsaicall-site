How our analysis works

SportsAICall publishes our opinion on upcoming cricket matches, informed by AI-driven statistical analysis. This page explains exactly what that means in practice — and just as importantly, what it doesn't mean.

01

What goes into every analysis

For every match we cover, our process looks at the same set of inputs, weighted by how relevant they are to that specific game:

Recent form

Each team's performance over their last 5–10 matches, not season-long averages that hide a hot or cold streak.

Head-to-head record

How these two sides have matched up historically, and in this format specifically.

Venue & conditions

Pitch tendencies at the ground, and how each side's strengths suit them.

Toss impact

How much winning the toss typically matters at this venue and format.

Squad & injuries

Confirmed playing XI where available, and the impact of any injuries or late changes.

Home / away

Where the match is being played, and each side's track record in these conditions.

02

How we present it

Every match write-up follows the same structure, on purpose — so it's easy to compare across matches and easy to check our work later:

  • An opinion statement in plain language — our read on the match in one or two sentences.
  • A probability split — e.g. Team A 60% / Team B 40% — never a flat "X will win."
  • The top reasons behind that view, drawn from the inputs above.
  • A confidence flag — High Medium Low — that drops whenever key information is missing, unusual, or unconfirmed (a brand-new playing XI, an unfamiliar venue, a long injury layoff).

04

What this isn't

Important

This is editorial sports analysis and opinion, not betting or fantasy-sports advice. We don't sell "tips" or "picks," and a probability is not a promise — sport is uncertain, and our confidence flag exists specifically to be honest about how much (or little) we actually know going into a match.
